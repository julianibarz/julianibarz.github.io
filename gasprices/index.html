What's behind the $6 gallon

The Bay Area $6 is California gasoline. Nationally, the $6 is diesel. They have different causes. Gasoline follows crude, and crude follows the Iran war. Diesel is short on its own, because the Gulf, Russia and China have all stopped exporting much of it at once.

The short answer

  1. Crude is up about $35 a barrel because of the Iran war. Brent was about $71 before the Feb 28 strikes and is about $107 today. That adds roughly 85¢ to every gallon of anything. Middle East flows through Hormuz are recovering, but trackers put them anywhere from 68% to 94% of normal. Shipping out of the Gulf now costs around $28–30 a barrel in freight alone.
  2. Diesel is a refining shortage on top of that. Gulf diesel exports are about a quarter of pre-war levels. Russia's diesel exports are down about 80%, from Ukraine's drone campaign plus Moscow's own export ban. China has cut fuel exports too. US refiners are running at 96–97% and shipping record volumes abroad, so US distillate stocks sit 12% below normal. The margin between crude and diesel is now about $2.67 a gallon, up from $0.85 a year ago.
  3. California's gas price is the national shock on top of a fixed premium. Since February, CA gasoline rose $1.56 and the US average rose $1.54. The CA premium over the US was about $1.50 before the war and is about $1.53 now. Taxes and climate programs account for about $1.20 of it. The two recent refinery closures account for most of the rest.
TL;DR 
• California gas (~$6.30) is the national shock on top of CA's usual ~$1.50 premium. Since February, CA gas rose $1.56 and the US average $1.54, so the CA premium barely moved.
• The shock is the Iran war: Brent went from ~$71 to ~$107, about 85¢ a gallon. Hormuz flows are recovering (68–94% of normal depending on the tracker), but Gulf freight costs ~$28–30 a barrel.
• Diesel ($6.45 US, $8.35 CA, both records) is a refinery shortage: Gulf diesel exports are ~¼ of pre-war, Russia's are down ~80%, China cut exports. The diesel refining margin is ~$2.67 a gallon vs $0.85 a year ago.
• Trump's Ukraine claim is partly true for diesel (~15–30% of the rise since February) and wrong for gasoline and crude. The Gulf lost 2–3x more diesel than Russia from March to August.
• Julian's Stahel point holds for diesel into winter: Russian refining is down about half and its export ban is being extended. For crude and gasoline, Iran still sets the price. Trump rejected Tehran's offer to reopen Hormuz on Sep 26.

Inside a gallon of diesel

Most of diesel's rise is refining margin, not crude. Over the past year the retail price rose $2.78. The margin between crude and diesel accounts for $1.82 of that and crude for $0.81. In September the NY Harbor diesel margin over WTI averaged about $110 a barrel, the highest monthly average in EIA data. The previous record was $95 in October 2022, and the norm before the war was $16–29.

Three bottlenecks, stacked

Each layer adds to the one before it. Crude sets the floor for every fuel. Refining capacity sets the diesel premium. California's market structure sets the extra $1.50 you pay in the Bay Area.

Easing, slowly

1. Crude: Hormuz and the Gulf

  • Brent at $106.87 and WTI at $94.85 on Sep 28. Brent futures peaked near $126 in April. Physical Dated Brent touched $130.80 on Sep 15, which is where the $130 talk comes from.
  • Gulf oil exports were about 13 mb/d in August, roughly half of pre-war (IEA). Kpler puts September at 12.8 of 18.8 mb/d.
  • Tankers now shuttle out under CENTCOM-coordinated transit windows. War-risk insurance runs 3–10% of hull value, against 0.2% before the war.
  • Saudi's Red Sea bypass pipeline was hit Sep 10–11. It restarted Sep 22 at about half capacity, with full recovery 6–8 weeks out. Aramco zeroed October crude to European term buyers.
  • US Strategic Petroleum Reserve is about 285 mb, the lowest since the early 1980s. SPR sales have ended.
Worst on record

2. Diesel: the world is short

  • Russia and Middle East diesel exports are down about 75% year over year. Nearly half of that drop is Russia (IEA, via Bloomberg's Javier Blas).
  • Russian refinery runs are down 30–50% depending on the tracker. Russia has banned diesel exports since Jul 8 and is set to extend the ban past Sep 30.
  • China's fuel stocks are at decade lows and Beijing is limiting exports. South Korea's gasoline exports fell by half.
  • US refiners are at their highest diesel run rates in 22 years and are exporting 1.7–1.9 mb/d. Distillate stocks are 12–13% under the five-year average.
  • Distillate stocks are the lowest for this time of year since 1982. The diesel margin over crude averaged about $110 a barrel in September, a record.
Structural, steady

3. California: an island market

  • Phillips 66 Los Angeles closed in Oct 2025 and Valero Benicia in early 2026. That is about 17% of state refining capacity gone.
  • CA now imports record volumes of gasoline and blendstocks, mostly from Asia, just as Asian exports collapsed.
  • Fixed costs of about $1.20 a gallon: state taxes and fees (74¢ incl. the 63.4¢ excise), cap-and-trade (~25¢), LCFS (~16¢), CARB-spec blend (~10¢).
  • GasBuddy blames this week's +14¢ on West Coast refinery snags. No specific outage has been reported publicly.
  • Newsom lifted the summer-blend rule statewide on Sep 28. That is worth roughly 10–15¢ over the next few weeks.
Fact-check

Is Ukraine bombing Russian refineries the cause?

"He has to stop knocking out diesel fuel in Russia."Trump to reporters at Doonbeg, Ireland, Sep 13, 2026

Partly true for diesel Wrong for gasoline and crude

The claim has a real core. Ukraine has knocked out enough Russian refining that Moscow banned diesel exports, and that pulled roughly 600,000 barrels a day of diesel off the world market this summer. Buyers in Europe and Latin America then bid for US Gulf Coast diesel, which pushes up the price at US pumps.

The claim goes wrong on scale. On Truth Social Trump said the diesel spike is mostly the Russia–Ukraine war and not Iran. The volumes say otherwise. From March to August the Gulf lost about 770,000 b/d of diesel exports against Russia's 350,000. By July–August the two were close to even. The IEA and the WSJ put Gulf losses at 2–3 times Russia's for the year. Crude is up because of Hormuz. Russia's refinery outages actually pushed more Russian crude onto the export market, which holds crude prices down a little.

The energy-strike truce Trump announced on Sep 14–15 did not hold. Both sides hit energy targets that same night, and Moscow's refinery was struck again on Sep 20.

Julian's question

If Iran has lost control of Hormuz, does Ukraine become the bigger issue?

Alexander Stahel (@BurggrabenH) argues that Gulf oil exports are back to about 94% of their January level. ADNOC, then Aramco and Kuwait, built what he calls a "Hormuz Shuttle": about 116 tankers run the Oman coast in CENTCOM-coordinated windows and transfer cargo ship-to-ship in the Gulf of Oman.

For diesel, mostly yes. Stahel's Sep 26 tally has Russian refinery runs at about 2.6 mb/d, down 51% from January, with 14 refineries offline and 17 strikes in the past four weeks. He expects no Russian diesel exports for the rest of 2026. If Gulf flows keep improving while Russia stays out, Russia's share of the diesel problem grows into winter.

For crude and gasoline, not yet. 94% is the most optimistic count available. Kpler has September at about 68%, the WSJ at about 80% including bypass routes, and EIA still expects 5.7 mb/d shut in during Q4. Stahel himself posted a follow-up the same day: the shuttle is inefficient and product exports remain disrupted, so cracks, diesel and gasoline stay high even if crude falls below $100. And the Iran track just got worse: Trump rejected Tehran's seven-day plan to reopen the strait on Sep 26, and aides expect strikes to resume after the Nov 3 midterms.

ClaimWhat the data says
"California closed 12 refineries in the past 12 months"False. Two closed: Phillips 66 Los Angeles (Oct 2025, 139k b/d) and Valero Benicia (early 2026, 145k b/d). Energy Secretary Wright's "two large refineries" is accurate. Martinez (2020) and Rodeo (2024) converted to renewable diesel earlier.
"It's Newsom, not the war"Mostly false for the recent spike. California's premium over the US moved about 2¢ since February. The closures did widen it by about 20¢ between Sep 2025 and Feb 2026, and they make CA more exposed to Asian import disruptions.
"It's Ukraine, not Iran"Partly true for diesel, false for gasoline and crude. See the fact-check above.
"Hormuz is back, so oil should crash"Flows are recovering, but estimates range from 68% to 94%. Freight and insurance add $28–30/bbl, and product exports lag crude. Brent rose back to $107 today after the Iran proposal was rejected.
"A diesel export ban will fix it"Unclear. Politico reported a 90-day ban plan on Sep 23 and ULSD futures fell 4%. The White House denied it, and Senate leader Thune says he is open to one. It would lower US diesel but push Latin America and Europe toward Russia, India and China.
